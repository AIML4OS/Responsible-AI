Legal acts, standards and reference materials

1 How to use the bibliography?

The sources below can serve as a starting point for deepening your knowledge of responsible AI, data protection, risk management and the use of artificial intelligence in official statistics.

When using legal sources, check the current wording of the provisions, the dates from which they apply, and any amendments.

2 1. European Union law

2.1 Artificial Intelligence Act

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Act).

The Act establishes a regulatory framework for AI systems, including a risk-based approach, the obligations of certain entities, and requirements for selected uses of AI.

  • Text of the Regulation in EUR-Lex

2.2 General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27 April 2016.

The GDPR sets out the rules for processing personal data, the rights of data subjects, and the obligations of controllers and processors.

  • Text of the GDPR in EUR-Lex

2.3 European Health Data Space and other sector-specific regulations

In projects that use sector-specific data, it is worth checking whether additional provisions on access to data, its re-use or its exchange apply.

  • EUR-Lex — access to European Union legal acts

6 5. Machine learning and validation

6.1 scikit-learn documentation — model validation

The materials describe data splitting methods, cross-validation, the selection of quality measures and typical problems in model evaluation.

  • scikit-learn — Cross-validation
  • scikit-learn — Model evaluation

The documentation concerns a Python library, but the validation principles it describes also apply when designing analyses in R and other environments.

6.2 tidymodels package documentation

Materials on modelling, data splitting, parameter tuning and model quality assessment in the R environment.

  • tidymodels — official website

6.3 forecast package documentation

Materials on modelling and forecasting time series, including the assessment of forecast quality.

  • forecast — R package documentation

7 6. Generative AI and language models

7.1 Vendors’ technical documentation

The documentation of generative tools can help in understanding their functions, limitations and settings. It does not, however, replace independent validation or a security assessment.

  • OpenAI — documentation
  • Microsoft — Responsible AI
  • Google — Responsible AI

When using these materials, pay attention to the publication date, the scope of the system described, and any changes in functionality.

8 7. How to assess the reliability of a source?

Before using a material in a project or in documentation, it is worth checking:

  • Author and institution — does the source come from the competent authority, a standardisation body or an academic author?
  • Date — is the information up to date?
  • Document status — is it a legal act, a standard, a recommendation, a technical document or an opinion piece?
  • Scope of application — do the conclusions concern a similar type of system and data?
  • Verifiability — is it possible to reproduce the results described or to check the provisions cited?

9 8. A note on currency

The bibliography is a starting point for further work. Before using a source in organisational procedures or in a conformity assessment, check the current edition of the document, its status and its scope of application.

In particular, obligations arising from EU AI legislation may depend on the type of system, the role of the organisation and the timetable for applying specific provisions.
